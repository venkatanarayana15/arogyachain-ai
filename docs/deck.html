The problem

Indian public facilities hold 17–51% of essential medicines.
The WHO benchmark is 80%.

  • Stock-outs run 4 to 14 weeks at a time
  • Stock ledgers are paper — district consolidation takes weeks
  • Districts learn about a shortage after patients are turned away

We are not short on procurement money. We are short on warning.

Cited: availability range and stock-out duration — “Factors Affecting the Availability and Utilization of Essential Medicines in India: A Systematic Review” (PMC11174260). 80% benchmark per WHO.

Scaling across India — and BRICS

New district, same code. The levers are configuration, not rewrites.

  • NLEM 2022 — the national standard, not a state list. 8 PHCs → 25,000: no architecture change
  • Language is a config pack. Tamil · Hindi · English shipped; 22 official languages follow the same Speech-to-Text + Gemini path
  • BRICS: the same forecasting and matching design applies to Brazil’s UBS, South Africa’s PHC clinics, and Russia’s outpatient network. Drug lists and languages swap; the Risk Score formula does not.

Our reachability sweep is the scaling law: the further a facility sits from a donor, the more forecasting must carry before transfer can help. That is the variable a national rollout would tune per state.
